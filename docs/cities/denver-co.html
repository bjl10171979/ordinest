Short-term rental alerts — Denver, CO

Denver requires every short-term rental to be licensed and to be the host's primary residence — and every advertisement must display the license number. Rules like these are exactly what changes quietly. Ordinest watches Denver's licensing pages daily.

Official sources we watch

  • Denver — Short-term rentals (Business Licensing) denvergov.org · Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection

What's in force right now

Standing rule

License required for stays under 29 nights

If you rent for 28 days or fewer at a time, a short-term rental license is required — and the unit must be your primary residence. Stays of 30+ days fall under Denver's residential rental property license instead.

Official source →
Standing rule

Ads must show the license number

All advertisements must display the host's business license number. Hosts must also follow safety, tax, zoning, and insurance rules — including at least $1 million in liability coverage (via the booking platform or your own policy).

Official source →
Standing rule — taxes

Lodger's tax is 10.75%

Denver's lodger's tax applies to every short-term stay under 30 consecutive days, collected at the time of sale. Hosts need both a business license and a lodger's tax license.
